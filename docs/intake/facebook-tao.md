## System in three lines
TAO ("The Associations and Objects") is the read-optimised, geographically distributed graph store that serves every node and edge of Facebook's social graph to the web tier. It replaced a PHP-plus-memcache lookaside architecture with a graph-aware two-level cache — many follower tiers in front of one leader tier per region — over statically sharded MySQL, so that the cache itself owns invalidation, thundering-herd protection and read-after-write consistency. It is hard because one page renders hundreds of graph items with per-viewer privacy checks, reads outnumber writes roughly 500:1, and the graph is too interconnected to partition by user, so every region must hold a complete multi-petabyte copy.

## Scale (only if stated)
- Users: "more than a billion active users" at the time of the 2013 paper (atc13-bronson.pdf §1)
- Requests per second (2013): "TAO can sustain a billion reads per second on a changing data set of many petabytes"; the deployment "continuously processes a billion reads and millions of writes per second" (atc13-bronson.pdf §1, §7)
- Requests per second (2020): "These user requests amplify to more than ten billion read queries per second to our online graph data stores, which also process tens of millions of writes per second" (https://www.usenix.org/system/files/osdi20-shi.pdf §8.1); restated as "over ten billion reads and tens of millions of writes per second" (https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol14/p3014-cheng.pdf §1)
- Query volume (2020): FlightTracker manages consistency "for billions of users and more than 10^15 queries per day" (osdi20-shi.pdf, abstract)
- Data volume: "many petabytes"; "each TAO follower must be local to a tier of databases holding a complete multi-petabyte copy of the social graph" (atc13-bronson.pdf §1, §4.5)
- Sharding: "TAO partitions data into hundreds of thousands of shards" (https://engineering.fb.com/2013/06/25/core-infra/tao-the-power-of-the-graph/)
- Read/write mix: reads 99.8% of requests, writes 0.2%; read misses by followers are "25 times as frequent as writes" (atc13-bronson.pdf Fig. 3, §4.5)
- Read mix: assoc_range 40.9%, obj_get 28.9%, assoc_get 15.7%, assoc_count 11.7%, assoc_time_range 2.8%. Write mix: assoc_add 52.5%, obj_update 20.7%, obj_add 16.5%, assoc_del 8.3%, obj_delete 2.0%, assoc_change_type 0.9% (atc13-bronson.pdf Fig. 3)
- Empty results: assoc_get found an association only 19.6% of the time, 31.0% of assoc_range calls had a non-empty result, only 1.9% of assoc_time_range calls returned any edges; 45% of assoc_count calls return zero while 1% return more than 500,000 (atc13-bronson.pdf §7)
- Cache hit rate: "TAO's overall hit rate for reads was 96.4%" (atc13-bronson.pdf §8)
- Follower node: 144 GB RAM, 2x Intel Xeon E5-2660 8-core at 2.2 GHz with Hyperthreading, 10 Gigabit ethernet; peak 15-minute throughput scales with hit rate to roughly 500,000–600,000 requests/sec near a 100% hit rate (atc13-bronson.pdf §8, Fig. 7)
- Availability: "Over a period of 90 days, the fraction of failed TAO queries as measured from the web server was 4.9 x 10^-6" (atc13-bronson.pdf §8)
- Regions (2020): stores are "deployed across over ten datacenter regions"; "More than 99% of cache and index queries are served without any cross-region communication" (osdi20-shi.pdf §8.1)
- Replication lag: "TAO's slave storage servers lag their master by less than 1 second during 85% of the tracing window, by less than 3 seconds 99% of the time, and by less than 10 seconds 99.8% of the time" (atc13-bronson.pdf §8)
- FlightTracker: "more than 100 million Ticket reads and 20 million Ticket writes per second"; client-observed read error rate 1.1 x 10^-7 over 30 days (osdi20-shi.pdf §8.2)
- Companion memcache deployment: "processing over a billion requests per second and storing trillions of items" (nsdi13-final170_update.pdf §1)
- Object/association payloads: objects may store 1 MB and associations 64 KB; the average association data size was 97.8 bytes for the 60.5% of associations that carried data, average object data size 673 bytes (atc13-bronson.pdf §7)

## Requirements stated or clearly implied
- Availability target: explicitly first — TAO "explicitly favors availability and per-machine efficiency over strong consistency"; "Two of the most important requirements for TAO are availability and performance. When failures occur we would like to continue to render Facebook, even if the data is stale." (atc13-bronson.pdf §1, §6). Measured client-observed query failure rate 4.9e-6 over 90 days.
- Consistency: eventual consistency by default; read-after-write within a single follower tier under at most one failure; one write primary per shard. "TAO provides eventual consistency by asynchronously sending cache maintenance messages from the leader to the followers." "In normal operation (at most one failure encountered by a request) TAO provides read-after-write consistency within a single tier." (atc13-bronson.pdf §4.4, §6.1). Individual reads may be marked **critical** and are then proxied to the master region (§6.1). Since 2016 read-your-writes is supplied externally, by FlightTracker Tickets, rather than by write-through caching (osdi20-shi.pdf §1, §2.1).
- Latency target: no stated SLO; the blog frames the budget as "a few hundred milliseconds" for the whole page. Measured client-observed read latency across the five read operations: cache hits p50 1.0–1.3 ms, average 2.3–3.2 ms, p99 24.8–32.8 ms; cache misses p50 5.0–8.2 ms, average 11.2–75.3 ms, p99 47.2–186.8 ms (atc13-bronson.pdf Fig. 8). Writes issued from a region 58.1 ms away from the database masters pay that inter-region round trip (Fig. 9).
- Ordering needs: association lists are kept in descending `time` order per (id1, atype); "Leaders serialize concurrent writes that arrive from followers"; there is no cross-shard order guarantee — write metadata "give[s] no order guarantees between shards" (atc13-bronson.pdf §3.4, §4.4; osdi20-shi.pdf §4)
- Replay or reprocessing needs: undeliverable invalidation and refill messages are queued to disk on the leader and replayed; the shared delete pipeline is replayable because it rides a reliable log — "allows mcsqueal to simply replay invalidations that may have been lost or misrouted"; a bulk shard-level invalidation is the recovery operation after permanent leader loss (atc13-bronson.pdf §6.2; nsdi13 §4.1)
- Retention: MySQL is the durable system of record; the caches are LRU with no stated TTL. The RefillLibrary metadata buffer keeps "approximately 3 minutes of writes from all regions" (vldb p3014 §4.3).
- Compliance / residency: not stated. Per-viewer privacy is enforced in the application tier at read time, not in TAO.

## Boundaries
- Regions / zones: multiple geographic regions, deliberately few — data centre locations are "clustered into only a few regions, where the intra-region latency is small (typically less than 1 millisecond)" (2013), growing to "over ten datacenter regions" (2020). Each region has one complete set of databases, one leader cache tier, and "at least two follower tiers" (atc13-bronson.pdf §7). By 2020, "Cache and indexing systems maintain asynchronously updated replicas in every region, while database replicas are present only in some regions" (osdi20-shi.pdf §8.1).
- Master/slave split: the master region is chosen **per shard**, not per region; "We prefer to locate all of the master databases in a single region." One cache server hosts many shards, so "a server may be both a master and a slave at the same time" (atc13-bronson.pdf §4.5).
- Cluster boundary inside a region (companion memcache layer): frontend clusters of web plus cache servers, plus a storage cluster holding the databases; a "regional pool" is one set of cache servers shared by several frontend clusters (nsdi13 §4).
- Trust zones: not discussed. Privacy checks run in the web tier — "we filter every item with privacy checks that take into account the current viewer" (atc13-bronson.pdf §2).
- Named subsystems: TAO (objects and associations), the follower/leader cache tiers, memcache with mcrouter and mcsqueal, Wormhole (pub-sub replication), FlightTracker and FT-ReverseIndex, RefillLibrary, Ent (higher-layer query framework), RAMP-TAO.
- Single logical instance: "TAO is deployed at Facebook as a single geographically distributed instance"; "Facebook has a single instance of TAO in production", multi-tenant across "thousands of data types".

## Failure modes, incidents, tradeoffs the source discusses
- **Why the lookaside cache had to go — three named defects.** "Inefficient edge lists: A key-value cache is not a good semantic fit for lists of edges; queries must always fetch the entire edge list and changes to a single edge require the entire list to be reloaded." "Distributed control logic: In a lookaside cache architecture the control logic is run on clients that don't communicate with each other. This increases the number of failure modes, and makes it difficult to avoid thundering herds." And expensive read-after-write consistency, which had forced the remote-marker scheme. (atc13-bronson.pdf §2.1)
- **Invalidate versus refill is a correctness fix, not an optimisation.** "Since we cache only contiguous prefixes of association lists, invalidating an association might truncate the list and discard many edges. Instead, the leader sends a refill message to notify followers about an association write." (atc13-bronson.pdf §4.4)
- **Consistency messages must ride behind replication, never ahead of it.** "These messages are delivered in a region immediately after a transaction has been replicated to a slave database. Delivering such messages earlier would create cache inconsistencies, as reading from the local database would provide stale data." (atc13-bronson.pdf §4.5)
- **Double invalidation is accepted as the price.** A forwarded write updates the local leader's cache immediately, so "followers will receive two invalidates or refills from the write, one that is sent when the write succeeds and one that is sent when the write's transaction is replicated to the local slave database." A version number makes the late one a no-op. (atc13-bronson.pdf §4.5)
- **Writes lost during a mastership switch are not retried.** "The master region is controlled separately for each shard, and is automatically switched to recover from the failure of a database. Writes that fail during the switch are reported to the client as failed, and are not retried." Slaves were promoted to master "0.25% of the time due to planned maintenance or unplanned downtime". (atc13-bronson.pdf §4.5, §8)
- **Leader failure trades consistency for availability, explicitly.** Followers reroute read misses straight to the database and writes to a random leader in the tier; "If the failing leader is partially available then followers may see a stale value until the leader's consistency is restored." The general policy: "TAO routes around the failures in a best effort fashion in order to preserve availability and performance at the cost of consistency." (atc13-bronson.pdf §6.2)
- **Permanent leader loss can strand followers with stale data.** "Note that a follower may be left with stale data if these messages are lost due to permanent leader failure. This problem is solved by a bulk invalidation operation that invalidates all objects and associations from a shard id." (atc13-bronson.pdf §6.2)
- **Tier failover breaks read-after-write.** "Note that failing over between different tiers may cause read-after-write consistency to be violated if the read reaches the failover target before the write's refill or invalidate." (atc13-bronson.pdf §6.2)
- **Values can go backwards in a slave region.** "if the post-changeset entry is evicted from cache and then reloaded from the database, a client may observe a value go back in time in a single follower tier. Such a situation can only occur if it takes longer for the slave region's storage server to receive an update than it does for a cached item to be evicted from cache, which is rare in practice." (atc13-bronson.pdf §6.1)
- **No atomicity across the two shards of a bidirectional edge.** "TAO does not provide atomicity between the two updates. If a failure occurs the forward may exist without an inverse; these hanging associations are scheduled for repair by an asynchronous job." (atc13-bronson.pdf §4.2)
- **High-degree objects defeat the cache.** Objects with more than the per-atype cap (typically 6,000) of associations are not fully cached, so "for high-degree objects these queries will always go to the database, because the queried id2 could be in the uncached tail of the association list." The fixes are client-side: choose the cheaper query direction using assoc_count, or bound the id2 search by the object's creation time. (atc13-bronson.pdf §5.4)
- **Consistent hashing creates hot spots; cloning and client caches fix them.** "this semi-random assignment of shards to cache servers can lead to load imbalance: some followers will shoulder a larger portion of the request load than others." Mitigations are shard cloning plus the access-rate-driven client-side cache and throttle. (atc13-bronson.pdf §5.3)
- **Two cache tiers exist because one large tier does not work.** "large tiers are problematic because they are more prone to hot spots and they have a quadratic growth in all-to-all connections." The split also preserves the property the design rests on: "having a single cache coordinator per database". (atc13-bronson.pdf §4.4)
- **The graph cannot be partitioned by user, so every region pays for a full copy.** "The social graph is tightly interconnected; it is not possible to group users so that cross-partition requests are rare. This means that each TAO follower must be local to a tier of databases holding a complete multi-petabyte copy of the social graph. It would be prohibitively expensive to provide full replicas in every data center." (atc13-bronson.pdf §4.5)
- **Failure detection is timeout-based and admits brown-outs.** "This simple failure detector works well, although it does not always preserve full capacity in a brown-out scenario, such as bursty packet drops that limit TCP throughput." (atc13-bronson.pdf §6.2)
- **Write-through read-your-writes eventually became unscalable — the 2016–2020 migration.** RYW "would be violated if any of the following were true: user requests were routed to another cluster of web servers; the mapping from web server cluster to L1 cache cluster was changed; queries were failed over to a stale replica; cache contents were lost". Moving L1 caches off per-web-cluster pairing (to cut the number of cached copies) broke exactly that stickiness, so FlightTracker Tickets replaced write-through as the RYW mechanism. (osdi20-shi.pdf §2.1)
- **Critical reads are a poor escape hatch.** "TAO marks these queries critical, routing them to the L2 cache in the region holding database primaries, near the base of the communication tree. This strategy has latency and availability drawbacks. It is also not tolerant of spiky workloads." (osdi20-shi.pdf §2.1)
- **Stronger consistency was explicitly rejected, and so was weaker.** "This led us to reject consistency levels in which all writes (linearizability) or most writes (causal consistency) missing from a stale replica need to be visible... We also rejected weaker models like bounded staleness that do not guarantee that a user sees their own writes, which are difficult to use correctly for an interactive application." (osdi20-shi.pdf §2.3). The blog states the same tradeoff bluntly: "For many of our products, TAO losing consistency is a lesser evil than losing availability." (engineering.fb.com TAO post)
- **The cost of eventual consistency, finally measured.** "1 in 1,500 batched reads reflects partial transactional updates" — fractured reads visible to applications. Cross-shard Ent changesets fail 2.22% of the time, and the 0.03% of write transactions that stall "increase the scope and duration of anomalies", accounting for 55% of observed anomalies. (vldb p3014 §1, §3)
- **Transactions were bolted on rather than built in.** RAMP-TAO was designed so that "non-transactional requests to TAO are virtually" unaffected and "over 99.93% of read transactions" avoid going cross-region or to the database, at "a modest 0.42% increase in memory overhead". "We prevent transactions from interfering with TAO's availability, durability, and replication processes." (vldb p3014 §1, §5)
- **Thundering herds in the companion memcache layer, quantified.** For a hot key set over one week, "Without leases, all of the cache misses resulted in a peak database query rate of 17K/s. With leases, the peak database query rate was 1.3K/s." (nsdi13 §3.2.1)
- **Never rehash away from a failed cache server.** "Such an approach risks cascading failures due to non-uniform key access frequency. For example, a single key can account for 20% of a server's requests. The server that becomes responsible for this hot key might also become overloaded." Gutter shunts that load to idle servers instead. (nsdi13 §3.3)
- **A single invalidation-stream outage is a regional blast radius.** The FlightTracker paper notes that with the old fixed topology "the outage of one database replica" propagated through the invalidation streams — one of the reasons the fixed communication tree was abandoned. (osdi20-shi.pdf §2.1)

## Unknowns
- The number of leader and follower servers, follower tiers per region, and regions is never given; only "thousands of machines", "at least two follower tiers" per region, and "over ten datacenter regions".
- How per-shard mastership is assigned and what control plane performs the automatic promotion — "a global configuration" is named but never described.
- Whether the 2013 leader/follower naming still maps exactly onto the 2020 "L1 cache / L2 cache" naming, and whether there is still exactly one leader tier per region.
- Cache sizing and TTLs: eviction is LRU with per-type arenas, but no TTL or per-tier memory figure is given for TAO itself.
- The Flash-based secondary caching attributed to the leader tier appears only in the blog post and is not described in any paper.
- What, if anything, replaced MySQL for parts of the graph: "it became easier to use alternate database technologies as the system of record for parts of the social graph, such as for data types that experience high write rates or limited lifetimes" — none are named.
- Wormhole's internals, ordering semantics and retention are cited, not described.
- No fetched first-party source covers TAO after 2021.
- Privacy enforcement, encryption, residency, and isolation between the "thousands of data types" sharing the single multi-tenant instance are not discussed.
- Whether RAMP-TAO was fully rolled out beyond the feasibility deployment the paper benchmarks.

## Candidates
- New kinds: none required. Every box maps to an existing catalogue kind (`cache`, `sql-database`, `app-server`, `change-data-capture`, `pubsub-topic`, `worker`, `coordination-service`, `database-proxy`, `search-index`, `load-balancer`, `api-gateway`). Note for the encoding pass: `graph-database` exists but is the wrong fit — TAO is a graph *API and cache* over sharded MySQL, not a graph storage engine.
- New properties on existing kinds:
  - `cache.tier` (l1 | l2 | follower | leader) and `cache.coordinatorPerShard` (boolean) — the single-coordinator property the whole design rests on
  - `cache.invalidationMode` (invalidate | refill | both) — TAO needs both, chosen by data shape
  - `cache.shardCloning` (boolean), `cache.hotItemFeedback` (boolean), `cache.arenaPartitioned` (boolean)
  - `cache.backupTier` (reference) — the configured failover tier
  - `cache.failoverStrategy` (spare-pool | rehash | direct-to-store)
  - `sql-database.mastershipGranularity` (shard | database | region)
  - `sql-database.replicationLagP99Seconds` (number)
  - `change-data-capture.carriesCacheInvalidation` (boolean) — invalidations riding the binlog
- New edge properties: `deliveryOrder` (afterReplication) for the "never deliver an invalidation ahead of its replication" constraint; `idempotent` (boolean); `versioned` (boolean)
- New requirement paths:
  - `consistency.readYourWrites` (none | perCacheTier | perSession)
  - `consistency.criticalReadEscapeHatch` (boolean)
  - `availability.degradedReadsAllowed` (boolean) — "continue to render Facebook, even if the data is stale"
  - `data.fullReplicaPerRegion` (boolean) — the consequence of a graph that cannot be partitioned
- New rules (name + one-line condition):
  - `invalidation-ahead-of-replication`: a cache-invalidation edge into a region that does not follow that region's replication edge
  - `cache-list-invalidated-not-refilled`: cache stores ordered list prefixes and its maintenance edge is invalidate-only
  - `no-single-cache-coordinator`: two or more cache nodes write the same shard of a store, losing thundering-herd and consistency guarantees
  - `cross-shard-write-without-repair`: a write touching two shards with no compensating repair worker declared
  - `sticky-session-required-for-ryw`: design claims read-your-writes but routing to the cache tier is not sticky and no write-tracking service exists
  - `write-forwarded-cross-region-without-per-shard-mastership`: writes forwarded to a remote primary while mastership is declared per-region rather than per-shard
  - `cache-failover-rehash`: cache failover declared as rehash-onto-survivors rather than a dedicated spare pool
  - `unbounded-fanout-node-uncached`: a cache with a per-key item cap and no declared strategy for keys above it
